Our story

Built out of
bitten ankles and stubbornness.

Breezeguard started as a ₱120 Repellent pad holder made from a milk jug in a Cagayan de Oro bedroom. Today it is a small student brand shipping to 36 provinces — and the idea has not changed at all.

The business

What we do, in one paragraph

Breezeguard designs and sells a DIY mosquito Repellent pad system: a small Chargeable holder that burns an ordinary mosquito Repellent pad and pushes the repellent through a pinwheel vent so it spreads across the whole room instead of climbing straight to the ceiling.

We are a home-based business. Every unit is assembled, quality-checked, and packed by our seven-member team in our Cagayan de Oro workshop, then handed to a courier the same day. We do not import white-label hardware — the shell, vent lid, and fan mount are our own files, printed and assembled locally, so we can keep the price down and the quality up.

Our customers are ordinary Filipino households: renters in Metro Manila who cannot rewire a room for a wall socket, families in the provinces who buy Repellent pads by the dozen, and anyone who has ever given up and sprayed repellent on their own legs at 2 a.m.

What drives us

Mission, vision, and the rules we don't break

Our mission

To make bite-free nights affordable for every Filipino household — using hardware we build ourselves and Repellent pads they can already buy at the sari-sari store.

Our vision

To become the default mosquito control brand in the Philippines: known for honest claims, quiet hardware, and parts that can be repaired instead of replaced.

Our promise

No fake "100% guaranteed" claims. We publish real burn times, real coverage, and a safety guide in English and Tagalog on every box.

How we got here

Six prints, one launch

Every version below exists somewhere in our workshop — we kept the rejects because they are a good reminder of how long it took.

2024

The milk-jug prototype

A cut-up plastic container with a fan taped underneath. It worked, sort of, but smoke pooled at the ceiling.

Late 2024

First 3D-printed shell

We drew the pinwheel lid in a CAD program and printed 20 of them. Six cracked in the heat, so we thickened the rim.

2025

Testing season

Two hundred hours of burn tests across six Repellent pad brands, plus humidity and power-cut tests during typhoon weeks.

Early 2026

Classic, Twin Duo and Mini

Three models launched, sold mostly through word of mouth and Facebook Marketplace.

Now

12,000 homes and counting

Nationwide shipping, 36 provinces covered, and a repair-first warranty so a dead fan does not mean a new unit.
